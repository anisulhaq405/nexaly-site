Travel Agency Business Plan: Booking Value, Fees & Cash

Build a travel agency business plan that separates trip booking value, client and supplier funds, earned agency fees, service hours and refund exposure. Includes a fictional example.

Example basis: Businesses, amounts, fees and outcomes in the worked examples are fictional planning assumptions in USD, prepared November 7, 2026. They are not market averages, observed customer results or income promises.

Specify the travel service and who receives the money

A travel agency business plan should define its customer, destinations, services, supplier relationships and money flow. A planning-only advisor, a package seller and an airfare ticket agent can have different duties and economics. This guide uses a fictional agency arranging trips with agreed service fees and assumed supplier commissions; it does not establish accreditation or permission to handle client money.

Map who contracts with the traveler, who is merchant of record, where payment goes, who owes each service and who handles changes or refunds. Verify the requirements for the actual activity and location. Do not describe a booking system or supplier relationship as active without evidence. The plan must keep customer travel funds separate from the agency's own earned income and operating forecast.

The $20,000 trip value is not $20,000 of agency operating income in this simplified agency model. The $2,000 commission is a fictional contract assumption, not a universal margin; its eligibility, base, timing and reversals must be checked. The $100 service fee is also invented and must be considered within actual agreements and applicable requirements.

Do not double-count a service fee embedded in the trip value and then add it again as separate income. This example explicitly treats the ten $100 fees as outside the $20,000 supplier-booking value. A different principal-versus-agent business model may require a different reporting boundary and professional assessment.

Count changes and support in service capacity

Assume each of the ten trips needs three hours to plan and arrange, plus one hour of expected changes or traveler support. That is 40 direct hours. With 60 total owner hours and 15 reserved for administration and supplier reconciliation, the direct allowance is 45 hours and the buffer is five.

Two disrupted itineraries needing four additional hours each raise direct work to 48 hours, three beyond the allowance. That is a staffing and service-coverage problem even if the original bookings looked profitable. Record travel dates, emergency coverage arrangements, handoffs and the boundaries of the promised service; a generic monthly average can conceal simultaneous departures.

Use the weekly time-blocking worksheet for manual support coverage; it is not a booking engine or travel alert service.

Build the cash bridge without spending client funds

Assume the agency has collected its $1,000 service fees but received only $800 of its $2,000 commission assumption this period. Its own operating receipts are $1,800; $1,200 remains expected later. Starting with $4,000 of operating cash and paying $1,100 of listed costs leaves $4,700. If the owner earmarks $600 for possible reversals or service refunds, unearmarked operating cash is $4,100. That earmark is an invented planning input, not a legal reserve rule.

Separately, suppose $18,000 due to suppliers is temporarily held pending onward payment. It must not be added to the $4,100 as a source of marketing or owner withdrawals. Maintain an appropriate reconciliation and follow the actual requirements. The California Attorney General seller-of-travel FAQs give a California-specific example of client-fund safeguards; they are not universal rules for every agency.

For a weaker-demand scenario of eight trips, modeled agency income becomes $2,400. Direct costs become $160 while overhead stays $900, leaving $1,340 before the same omissions. Separately model a delayed commission or a reversal after a cancellation. Lower volume and slower collection are different stresses.

Document refund responsibilities and the review loop

The US DOT refund guidance explains ticket-agent responsibilities within its airfare scope, including the importance of merchant-of-record status. Do not turn that into a blanket rule for every hotel, package, service fee or jurisdiction. Check the actual booking and refund conditions, responsible party and applicable requirements before accepting payment.

Keep a dated evidence index for supplier terms, client consent, disclosures, service scope, money handling and expected commission statements. During a limited trial, reconcile booking IDs, changes, support hours, fees earned, money collected and open obligations. Store traveler identity and payment records in controlled systems, not a public business plan.

Use the business-plan guide for the wider structure and startup checklist for evidence owners. Is booking value the same as income? Not in this model. Is expected commission already available cash? No. Does a planning worksheet establish refund compliance? No; actual responsibilities need their own assessment.
