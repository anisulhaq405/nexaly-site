With B3 set to October 12, 2026, the Economics essay due October 16 shows 4 days to due and 3 hours left. The Statistics problem set due October 14 shows 2 days and 2 hours left. The Marketing presentation marked Done has no days-to-due value.

Capture the assignment details once

Start with each course syllabus and the current assignment instructions. Record the course, task, due date, submission time or timezone where relevant, estimated work, status and next action.

If a deadline is provisional, mark it as such. If a course announcement changes it, update the tracker and keep a note of the source. Do not assume an imported or copied date remains current throughout the semester.

For assignments with several stages, record meaningful milestones separately or put them in the task notes. A proposal, draft and final submission can have different dates. Treating all of them as one final deadline hides earlier work.

Keep unconfirmed deadlines out of the dated queue

If the syllabus says “date to be confirmed,” keep the assignment in a separate needs-confirmation list with its course, source and next action. Do not invent a due date to make the tracker accept a row. Check the current course announcement or ask the instructor, then move the task into the dated queue once the date, time and relevant timezone are verified.

For example, a lab report with no confirmed deadline can still have a next action: organize the observations and check the course announcement. Recheck this undated list at weekly review so sorting dated work does not hide it.

Build an upcoming assignments timeline

Start with unfinished assignments that have confirmed due dates. Sort by due date, then check the exact submission time and time zone in your course system. Put an earlier start date beside each deadline; the due-date order alone does not tell you which task needs the first study session.

For example, a 30-minute quiz due Wednesday and a six-hour paper due Friday can require work on the paper first. Reserve two hours on Monday, two on Tuesday and two on Thursday if those sessions fit your timetable. Keep Wednesday's quiz session visible as a separate commitment. This is a fictional planning example, not an automatic schedule produced by the download.

Review remaining hours and the next action after each session. Move a completed assignment out of the upcoming queue only after checking the submission receipt. Separate the deadline from the start date

The due date is when the work must be submitted. The start date is when you plan to begin. A large task can require several sessions before the deadline, so a due-date-only list may look manageable until the last day.

For a writing assignment, next actions could be selecting a question, finding suitable sources, outlining an argument, drafting and reviewing. For a problem set, the first action might be attempting the questions you can solve and marking those needing help.

Estimate effort to allocate time, not to judge yourself. Revise the estimate after the first session if the task is larger than expected. Include revision and submission checks rather than assuming the first draft is the final step.

Read the tracker flags correctly

If you build a day-based spreadsheet, choose a reference date and define how many days count as approaching. The downloadable workbook uses the reference date in B3 and calculates days to the deadline. It does not automatically label an approaching deadline. The free browser planner instead uses the explicit reference time you enter.

For that day-based sheet, you can label completed work Submitted, unfinished work before the reference date Overdue, and work due on that date Today. Label later work Soon or Upcoming using your chosen window. Check these rules after adding formulas; the free browser tool has its own displayed fields and results.

These flags organize the queue; they do not decide academic importance. A large assignment due later may need attention before a short task due sooner. Use the next-action and estimated-work fields together with the date flag.

The on-page example and free browser tool do not pull changes from Canvas, Blackboard or another learning system. Update the reference date and records as part of your review routine.

Keep a short review habit

At the start of a working session, choose a concrete next action that fits the time available. At the end, record what changed and write the next step while the task is still clear.

Review the upcoming week regularly. Check new announcements, changed instructions, overlapping deadlines and tasks waiting for feedback. After submission, confirm that the correct file or response appears in the required system; a checkbox in your own tracker is not a submission receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Is an assignment tracker different from a calendar?

A calendar shows when something happens. A tracker can also hold status, estimated work, sources and next actions. You can use both: maintain the task details in the tracker and put selected work sessions on your calendar.

Should I track every small reading task?

Track enough to avoid missing required work without making the system harder to maintain than the work itself. Group predictable small tasks when doing so keeps the instructions clear.

What if my professor changes a deadline?

Update the date from the current official course communication and adjust the affected work sessions. Keep the relevant source or note so you can verify it later.

Can the template improve my grades automatically?

It organizes work and deadlines. Learning, completing the assignment and meeting course requirements still depend on the work you do and the support you use.

Example: two assignments due on Friday

Suppose a biology worksheet needs two hours and an essay needs six hours. Both are due Friday at 11:59 p.m., but Thursday has only 90 free minutes. A tracker that records only the due date hides the workload. Record the time estimate, divide the essay into smaller sessions and schedule its first session earlier in the week.

Five tracker fields to review

  1. Course and assignment: recognizable course and task names.
  2. Verified due date and time: copied from the current course portal; note time zone where relevant.
  3. Next action: “outline introduction,” not just “work on essay.”
  4. Hours remaining: revise the estimate as work progresses.
  5. Status: distinguish not started, in progress, submitted and receipt confirmed.

If a professor changes the deadline, preserve the original date in notes and update the confirmed due date. If a deadline is unknown, label it TBD; do not invent a date simply to make a spreadsheet sort.

How to set up a Google Sheets assignment tracker without missing a deadline

Competitor templates commonly flag overdue rows, days remaining and completion status. Those signals are useful only when the due date is a real date value rather than text and the status matches what you submitted. The downloadable Excel and Google Sheets options linked earlier in this guide are the starting point; the formulas below are an optional custom-sheet example, not a claim that every supplied workbook has these exact columns.

ColumnHeadingExample row 2
ACourseBiology 101
BAssignmentLab report
CDue date2026-11-06 (enter as a date)
DStatusIn progress
EHours remaining4
FDays left=IF(C2="","",INT(C2-TODAY()))
GAction flag=IF(C2="","Add due date",IF(D2="Done","Complete",IF(C2<TODAY(),"Overdue",IF(C2=TODAY(),"Due today","Upcoming"))))

Copy F2 and G2 downward for additional rows. Set column C to Format → Number → Date in Google Sheets, and confirm dates against the syllabus. If Google Sheets displays a formula parse error because of your spreadsheet locale, its argument separator may differ; adjust the delimiter according to your locale settings.

What an overdue flag can and cannot tell you

  • Due date has passed, status still In progress: investigate the submission immediately; a red flag is not proof your instructor marked it late.
  • Status Done, but no submission receipt: keep the receipt or LMS confirmation separately. A spreadsheet checkbox is not submission evidence.
  • Two tasks due Friday: compare remaining hours with your actual available study blocks. Begin the larger task before Friday, even if both show the same days-left number.
  • Deadline changed: update the verified date and keep a short note of the instructor's announcement; do not trust an old exported sheet without review.
