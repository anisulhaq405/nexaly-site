Consulting Business Plan: Scope, Delivery Hours & Cash

Write a consulting business plan around a clear client problem, accepted deliverables, project hours, retainer capacity and payment timing. Includes an original worked model.

Example basis: Businesses, amounts, fees and outcomes in the worked examples are fictional planning assumptions in USD, prepared November 7, 2026. They are not market averages, observed customer results or income promises.

Define the consulting promise and acceptance evidence

A consulting business plan should explain which client problem you solve, the bounded result you deliver, who accepts it and what evidence supports demand. This guide models a small advisory practice with fixed-fee projects and limited retainers. It does not assume that an interest call is a signed engagement or that a persuasive proposal guarantees a result.

For example, define a stock-record review for a particular kind of small retailer: a written findings report, an agreed number of meetings and a handover checklist. Identify what is excluded, what data the client supplies and what happens if that data arrives late. Record permission to use client information and separate public planning notes from confidential source files.

Build one pipeline and a delivery-hour ceiling

Keep each opportunity under one ID with its current stage, next action, owner and decision date. A meeting, proposal and signed project can be stages of the same opportunity; adding those stage totals would overstate the pipeline. Validate demand with authorized conversations, written scope and actual paid pilots rather than generic market-size figures.

The 12-hour buffer is not another sale already made. If each project needs four extra hours, all 12 hours disappear. If each needs eight extra hours, total delivery rises to 116 hours and exceeds the allowance by 12. Retainers need a written hour or deliverable boundary; otherwise a fixed monthly fee can hide an unlimited service promise.

The $1,800 and $500 fees are fictional negotiated inputs, not industry prices. The $4,560 remainder excludes owner compensation, tax and costs not listed. Do not call it take-home pay or compare it with another practice that includes owner salary. Keep subcontractor costs, reimbursed travel and client-paid software inside a consistently defined boundary.

A project with the same fee but more revisions uses more capacity without automatically earning more income. Review change requests before committing extra hours. Use the scope-change worksheet to record changed work and the agency capacity example for a broader team allocation.

Put deposits and later balances on different dates

Assume each project collects a 50% deposit and both retainers are paid at the start of the month. Initial receipts are 3 x $900 + 2 x $500 = $3,700. The other $2,700 of project fees is expected after the agreed work and payment conditions. With $3,000 opening operating cash and all $1,840 of listed costs paid before those balances arrive, the intermediate balance is $4,860. It is not the eventual revenue total.

If only two projects are delivered alongside the same two retainers, modeled revenue becomes $4,600. Direct costs fall to $320, while listed overhead stays $1,400; the remainder becomes $2,880 before the same omissions. Separately test a late balance: earned income can remain unchanged while the receipt moves into another month. A positive margin does not remove a cash deadline.

Turn the plan into a bounded evidence test

Write a short customer definition, service scope, alternatives, lead channel, delivery calendar, fee basis, cash schedule and downside response. The business-plan guide connects these sections; the editable template captures the narrative. The SBA planning resource is a starting point for choosing the document format.

During a 30-day pilot, retain the original estimates and record actual preparation, meetings, analysis, revisions and collection dates. Set a spending ceiling and name the person who decides whether to continue, narrow the offer or stop. A consulting niche may involve regulated professional activity; verify the requirements for the actual work and location before offering it. This worksheet does not establish a credential or interpret a client contract.

Should every available hour be billable? No; reserve the non-delivery commitments first. Is a retainer guaranteed recurring profit? No; renewal, scope, cost and collection remain assumptions. What belongs in an investor or lender version? Confirm the recipient's additional evidence and statement requirements rather than assuming this short model is sufficient.

A one-page consulting plan you can actually test

Use the existing three-project model as the financial appendix to a short written plan. The sales assumptions must support those three projects without using the same opportunity twice. The following funnel is a separate fictional acquisition test, not a benchmark or a prediction.

Buyer and problemOne buyer role, business type and costly problem; retain dated interview notes.

Sales test30 qualified prospects → 12 calls → 6 proposals → 3 signed projects.
DeliveryThe 92 committed hours above, the 12-hour buffer and a change-request owner.
Money and decisionStartup outlays, dated collections, payment commitments and the evidence needed to continue.

The assumed stage conversions are 40%, 50% and 50%: 30 × 0.40 × 0.50 × 0.50 = 3 projects. Six proposals at a 25% win rate would produce an expectation of 1.5 wins, not three bookings. Keep actual signed work separate from probability-weighted forecasts, and avoid spending against fractional expected clients.

Use the editable business plan template to capture the narrative. If the sales test succeeds but hours overrun, revise the role and delivery-capacity plan before promising a fourth project. A lender version may need a fuller financial package; the SBA plan outline helps identify that additional scope.
