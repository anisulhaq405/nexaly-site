Start with the relationship task

A CRM organizes contacts, organizations, interactions and deals. Choosing one should begin with the records and actions your team must maintain, not a claim that every business needs the largest feature set. This documentation-based comparison, reviewed November 12, 2026, identifies what to inspect in HubSpot, Zoho CRM and Pipedrive. It is not a paid-account test, current-price quote or universal best-software ranking.

Use the solopreneur CRM guide for a single-owner routine. The CRM automation guide owns field authority and safe transitions. This page owns software evaluation, including administration and a future exit.

Build an acceptance pack before importing customer data

Create a harmless test organization with two contacts and one deal. Record a conversation, assign a next action, change a stage with evidence and export the data. Confirm that the contact-to-organization and deal relationships survive the export or a test restore. A CSV containing names alone may not preserve the record history you need.

Try a duplicate import, a shared address and a changed contact detail. Keep ambiguity in a review queue. Confirm that ordinary users can do their work while restricted records and administrative settings follow the intended permission design. Do not assume an integration label proves every field or action is supported.

Calculate total seat cost and operating effort

Fictional comparison, not named vendor pricing: a three-person team considers option A at $20 per paid seat per month, plus a $15 monthly add-on. Annual subscription cost is 3 × $20 × 12 + $15 × 12 = $900. An assumed six setup hours at $30 add $180, making first-year planning cost $1,080.

Option B has no assumed subscription charge but requires three extra administration hours each month at $30, or $1,080 annually, before setup. This is not an argument that paid software always saves money. It shows why scope, labor assumptions and the exact usable offer matter. If saved hours do not reduce expenditure, describe capacity rather than claiming a cash saving.

Separate pipeline value, access and exit requirements

A deal value is not a receipt. Keep invoice and collection evidence in the appropriate system and use a cash forecast for timing. The sales automation guide explains why expected pipeline cannot replace a full-win capacity test.

Before relying on a CRM, retain export instructions, required record types, attachment handling, relationship identifiers and the owner responsible for a move. Rehearse with harmless records rather than discovering the exit format when a contract ends. Check current terms, retention and access settings for your actual use; a provider's broad security claim is not an account-specific permission review.

Make the choice reviewable

Mark every mandatory requirement pass, fail or unverified. Consider ease of updating records alongside admin workload, adoption and support. Run a small pilot with an agreed cutoff and reconcile imported rows and destination records. Avoid an irreversible bulk migration before the identity policy and backup procedure have been tested.

Frequently asked questions

Is a free CRM enough? It can be if the actual limits and required functions fit your task; check the current edition. Can offline NexalyPlanner backups import into these CRMs? Do not assume compatibility; no such connector is supplied here. What matters most? Reliable records, a maintainable next-action routine, an acceptable cost boundary and a demonstrated exit path.
