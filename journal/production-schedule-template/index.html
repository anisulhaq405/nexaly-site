Production Schedule Template for a Small Workshop

By NexalyPlanner · Published October 20, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026

Back to the NexalyPlanner Journal

A production schedule template turns orders into dated work on a named person, machine or workstation. For a small workshop, start with the required quantity, due date, material readiness, setup time, run time and review time. Then check that the work fits the available hours on each resource, not just the weekly total.

Free starter: Download the printable production schedule template. Open the HTML file in a browser and print it, or choose Save as PDF. It includes a weekly resource plan and a job-detail page. It does not automatically calculate dates, move jobs or reserve materials.

The example below follows a fictional canvas-accessories workshop with one sewing workstation. All times and quantities are planning assumptions. Use your own observed process times before making delivery commitments. This is a manufacturing work schedule; for social posts and videos, use the separate content production capacity guide.

Choose a weekly view and a job-detail row

Use the weekly page to see where work is booked. Give each resource its own row per day. Keep the detail page for the job ID, SKU, quantity, material check, predecessor task, setup, production, inspection and packing. A colored calendar block without these assumptions can look feasible while hiding several hours of unfinished work.

Smartsheet's production scheduling template collection distinguishes daily, weekly and finite-capacity schedules. The original starter here deliberately uses a small weekly layout. It suits a few jobs sharing limited resources, not a factory-wide scheduling engine.

Calculate available time before placing orders

The workshop opens for eight hours each weekday. In this example, breaks, routine maintenance and administration remove two hours from the sewing station's planned day. That leaves six hours Monday through Friday, or 30 hours for the week. Do not count the same person's time twice just because two machines are available.

Write the daily deductions explicitly. If a machine service takes another two hours on Wednesday, Wednesday capacity becomes four hours and the weekly total becomes 28. A maintenance appointment is not extra work that can sit outside a fully booked calendar. The equipment maintenance due planner can organize due work; it does not reserve production time automatically.

Use one time unit throughout. If the run rate is three minutes per item, 60 items require 180 minutes, or three hours. For this example, planned job hours equal setup hours plus quantity multiplied by minutes per unit divided by 60, plus the separate inspection and packing allowance.

Worked production schedule: three jobs, one station

Assume all three jobs use the same workstation and operator during their stated setup, run and check time. Materials for J-101 and J-103 are ready Monday. J-102's fabric is due Tuesday but must be received and checked before that job starts. The workshop leaves its customer dispatch commitments on the order record.

Mark Wednesday's J-102 booking as conditional until the material check is complete. A supplier's promised delivery date is not proof that usable fabric is available. Track receipts with the partial shipments and backorders workflow, including what remains outstanding.

Test a rush order against the actual day

A customer asks for a new job requiring three hours on Monday, with finished goods needed Tuesday morning. Monday has only two unallocated hours. The proposed load is 4 + 3 = 7 hours against six available hours, so it is one hour over capacity. The week's 17 spare hours do not solve a Monday conflict.

Record an explicit decision: change the dispatch promise, move another job with approval, use a genuinely available qualified resource, or decline the rush date. Do not silently assume overtime or shorten an inspection allowance. If you move part of a job to Tuesday, check the customer's required finish time and whether stopping creates another setup.

For example, splitting a run across two days might add a 0.25-hour restart that the original estimate omitted. Record it as a new assumption and recalculate the affected day. The template does not decide which jobs may safely pause; that depends on your process.

Keep material readiness and lot records separate

For each job, verify the required materials, available quantities, condition and the version of the work instruction. A material reservation is a planning commitment, not proof that every component is physically on the shelf. Where inventory looks uncertain, use an inventory count sheet before confirming the start.

Record a material or finished-goods lot reference when your operation uses one. The production schedule says when work is planned; the batch and lot log records what was actually used, produced and shipped. Keep the connection without treating the calendar as a replacement for traceability records.

Capacity check before promising a rush job (fictional): A workstation has 8 scheduled hours, 1 hour of planned downtime and 1 hour of already committed setup, leaving 6 hours available. If two existing orders require 4.5 hours of remaining run time, only 1.5 hours are uncommitted. A rush job requiring 2 hours would exceed that capacity by 0.5 hour unless work is rescheduled, capacity is added or the delivery date changes. Record the decision and its impact on earlier promises.

Update the schedule using completed work

Suppose J-101 actually takes 4.75 hours and produces 58 accepted pouches with two awaiting rework. Record all three facts: actual time, accepted quantity and unresolved quantity. The remaining Monday capacity is then 6 − 4.75 = 1.25 hours, not the original two. Sixty units sewn does not mean 60 units ready for dispatch.

If the two pieces need an estimated 0.5-hour rework session on Tuesday using S1, Tuesday's booked load rises from 3.5 to four hours. Keep the original plan and enter the revision with its reason. Confirm the accepted quantity and order requirement before promising that the job is complete.

At the daily review, ask which jobs finished, which start conditions remain open and which dates changed. Carry unfinished work forward as remaining work, rather than copying the original full job hours into every day. This avoids counting hours already consumed as future capacity requirements.

Reschedule a partial material receipt using remaining work

J-102 originally needs 40 totes, with 0.75 hours of setup, four hours of running time and 0.75 hours of checking and packing: 5.5 hours total. Suppose only enough checked fabric for 30 totes is ready. A supplier's promise for the balance does not make all 40 ready to produce.

If your process permits splitting the job, the first batch can use 0.75 setup + 30 × 6 ÷ 60 running + 0.75 checking = 4.5 hours. A later ten-unit batch might need 0.25 restart + one running hour + 0.25 checking = 1.5 hours. Together the split uses six hours, half an hour more than the original uninterrupted job. The additional restart and checking allowances are explicit new assumptions, not automatic template calculations.

Put the remaining batch on a date when the materials and the same required resource are available. Record 30 accepted units only after their check; keep the ten-unit commitment open. Do not copy the full original 5.5-hour job into both dates, or label the customer order complete because the first batch finished.

Review both the constraint and its owner

Use the PO receipt record for accepted material quantities and the supplier review for recurring delivery issues. Keep the customer's dispatch date in the order tracker. These records support the schedule; the printable HTML file does not import them or recalculate a sequence.

If a split is not suitable for your process, retain the job as blocked and review the delivery promise. A weekly total of spare hours cannot remove a material or dated-resource conflict.

Questions before using the template

Is this a master production schedule?

No. It is a short-range resource and job worksheet. It does not calculate demand forecasts, bills of materials, purchasing requirements or optimized sequences.

Can I use it for more than one workstation?

Yes. Copy the resource rows and check each resource independently. When one operator serves both stations, also check that person's schedule; adding machine hours together can hide a labor conflict.

Does the download save my entries?

The download is a blank printable HTML worksheet. Write on the printed pages or annotate a saved PDF using your own application. It has no browser storage or automatic calculations. Keep the completed version with a date and job references.

How to calculate a small workshop production schedule from available capacity

Start with confirmed orders, materials available and realistic machine or labor hours. As an illustration, ten jobs needing two labor-hours each require 20 labor-hours before setup and rework; two workers with six productive hours available per day provide 12 worker-hours daily, so completing everything in one day is not realistic. Schedule the constrained tasks first and revise dates when materials arrive late. Keep purchase and inventory records separate from finished production. Use the Supplier Lead Time Log to review delivery assumptions and the Purchase Order Template for ordering missing materials.
