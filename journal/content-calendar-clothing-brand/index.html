A content calendar for a clothing brand should connect each post to a product, a customer question and a next step. Planning only “Reel on Tuesday” leaves the most important decisions unfinished: what the shopper will learn and what they can do afterward.

The 30-day example below gives a small boutique a complete starting calendar. It combines product details, styling, behind-the-scenes work and purchase information. These are editorial suggestions, not a proven posting frequency or a guarantee of reach.

Choose a small product focus for the month

Start with a manageable group of products that you can actually show and sell. This example uses an everyday tee, a lightweight layer, a dress and a scarf. Replace those examples with your current stock.

For each product, collect the details shoppers repeatedly ask about: measurements, available colors, fabric, care information and delivery or pickup options. Use your actual product records. Do not invent a fabric property or fit claim because it sounds appealing in a caption.

Give each post one purpose. A detail demonstration can reduce uncertainty; a styling post can show a use case; a restock announcement can help a waiting customer act. A single post does not need to perform every job.

Use five practical content pillars

The calendar labels each post with one of five pillars:

  • Product clarity: show the item and answer a buying question.
  • Styling: demonstrate a practical way to wear or combine products.
  • Trust: show a real process, approved customer contribution or clear policy.
  • Conversation: ask a question you can use to improve the shopping experience.
  • Offer: explain a genuine launch, restock or purchase opportunity.

This is a planning framework, not a fixed ratio prescribed by a platform. If customers keep asking about sizing, give product clarity more space. If the month has no launch, replace launch posts with useful evergreen demonstrations.

Turn the calendar into a manageable production routine

Batch posts that use the same product setup. For example, one clear-light filming session can capture garment measurements, fabric texture, fastening details and color comparisons. Give every clip a descriptive filename so you can find it later.

Before editing, write the exact question the clip answers. Then select the shortest footage that answers it clearly. This prevents a calendar full of attractive videos that leave the customer uncertain about the item.

If daily publishing is unrealistic, use three or four entries per week and extend the plan. Keep the sequence's variety instead of squeezing too many posts into a single day. “30-day calendar” describes the supplied plan, not a requirement to publish every day.

Record a destination and a result

For each entry, add the product URL, owner, planned date and status. After publishing, record the actual post link and one relevant outcome, such as product-page visits, qualified questions or attributable orders.

Treat attribution carefully. A customer may see several posts and buy later. A manually entered sales note can be useful, but it should not be presented as proof that one post caused the purchase.

Keep ideas and scheduling separate

An idea bank holds possible angles. A calendar holds selected posts with a date, asset and purpose. Move only ideas you can produce into the committed schedule.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a clothing brand post?

Choose a pace you can sustain with useful, accurate material. Start with a manageable schedule, review the results and adjust. This calendar does not establish an ideal frequency for every brand or platform.

Can a small boutique reuse one product across several posts?

Yes, when each post answers a different question. Measurements, fabric detail, care and styling are distinct angles. Repeating the same sales caption without adding information provides less value.

What if I do not have customer photos?

Use your own product demonstrations, process footage and clear explanations. Do not fabricate testimonials or imply that a sample image came from a customer.

Does the download post to Instagram or TikTok?

No. The workbook organizes your plan. Publish through your chosen platform or a separately verified scheduling tool.
