A homeschool attendance tracker helps you keep a dated record for each child without reconstructing the month from memory. A practical starting format is one row per child per date, with a participation note and any time information you choose or are required to maintain.

The template in this guide is a general record-keeping aid. It does not decide what counts as an instructional day or establish a required number of hours. Check the current guidance that applies to your state and situation. The US Department of Education state resource directory is a starting point for state-specific information.

Download the free homeschool attendance tracker and open Attendance. The sheet includes fictional examples, blank rows, child-level summaries and a duplicate-entry flag.

Decide what your record means before entering data

Define the period you are tracking and the child labels you will use. Keep each label consistent, whether it is a name or an internal reference such as Child A.

The starter sheet includes a Day recorded? field with Yes and No options. “Yes” means you have chosen to record that date for that child under your applicable process. It is not an automatic judgment about legal attendance.

Use the activity note to explain what happened. If you record minutes, enter the actual time for the activities your record covers. The workbook does not convert a short activity into a full day or assume all family members participated identically.

Use one row per child per date

A daily row keeps the record easy to inspect. In this fictional example, two learners have different entries on the second date:

These entries produce two recorded days for Child A and one for Child B. Their listed minutes total 170 and 100 respectively. The times are sample data, not recommended daily schedules or statements about required instruction.

If you prefer one row per activity, use a different structure that summarizes unique child-and-date combinations. Counting every activity row as another attendance day would inflate the total.

Keep attendance, lesson completion and grades separate

An attendance entry records participation according to your chosen process. A lesson record describes the work. An assessment record describes an evaluation. The same child may participate in a day without finishing every planned lesson.

You can connect those records with a date and child label, but do not make one field stand in for all three. For example, a checkmark in an attendance column does not show which math skill was practiced.

For planning the work itself, use the multi-age homeschool schedule examples. They offer ways to arrange shared and individual lessons while keeping each learner's record distinct.

Add entries while the details are still clear

Choose a short daily check-in to record completed activities and any relevant notes. Update the entry when plans change rather than copying the original schedule as if everything happened exactly as planned.

At the end of the week, look for blank dates that need an explanation, inconsistent child labels and repeated rows. Do not fill an uncertain gap with invented detail. Use an honest note and consult whatever contemporaneous records you actually have.

The template's duplicate flag looks for the same child and date more than once. Resolve the repeated entry before relying on that child's day total. The summary displays a review message for a child with a flagged duplicate instead of silently presenting an inflated day count.

Make the multi-child summary understandable

The right-hand summary in the Attendance sheet groups records by the child label you enter. Keep those summary labels aligned with the labels in the log. “Child A” and “A” are different entries to a spreadsheet.

The recorded-day total counts rows marked Yes after the duplicate check. The minutes total sums the minutes entered for the child; it does not determine whether those minutes satisfy a rule.

The starter formulas cover the displayed entry area. If you add rows beyond it, extend the table and summary formulas together. Make a backup before changing the structure, then test with a known pair of records.

Keep records readable when you save or print them

Include the child's identifier, the record period and a clear explanation of the fields. Before sharing or printing, inspect the page breaks and confirm that the dates and labels remain visible.

Keep the working file separate from a dated archive. Use storage and sharing arrangements appropriate to the information in your records. A file saved on one device still needs a recovery plan.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use one attendance sheet for several children?

Yes. Give every row a consistent child identifier and summarize each child separately. Avoid copying the same participation or minutes into every child's record without checking what each actually did.

Should a field trip be recorded?

Record the actual activity and relevant details in your learning records. Determine how it should be classified using the requirements that apply to your situation; the template does not make that decision.

Is there one homeschool attendance requirement across the USA?

Use current state-specific guidance. This general template deliberately does not set a universal day or hour target.

Why does the summary say to review a duplicate?

The same child and date appear more than once in the entry area. Check whether the rows describe the same daily record, then consolidate or correct them before using the total.

Is a daily attendance log the same as a transcript?

No. They organize different information. Keep attendance, coursework and any transcript-related records in formats appropriate to their purpose.
