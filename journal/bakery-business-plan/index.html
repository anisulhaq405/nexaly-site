Write a bakery business plan using batches, saleable yield, sell-through, production time, ingredient costs and cash timing. Includes an original downside and stock reconciliation.

Example basis: Businesses, amounts, fees and outcomes in the worked examples are fictional planning assumptions in USD, prepared November 7, 2026. They are not market averages, observed customer results or income promises.

Choose the bakery format and selling unit

A bakery business plan starts with the actual operation: a home-based order business, neighborhood shop, wholesale producer and cafe have different capacity, delivery and regulatory needs. This guide models a fictional bread-batch operation selling one clearly defined unit. It does not provide a food-safety process, recipe approval or permission to sell a particular product.

Define the buyer, product range, pack/unit size, order channel, production window, delivery or collection arrangement and evidence of demand. Start with a bounded product mix before multiplying expected sales across many lines. A busy display or social audience does not establish paid sell-through, and unsold units are not revenue.

Production yield is 380 / 400 = 95%. Sell-through of saleable output is 342 / 380 = 90%. Sold share of all produced units is 342 / 400 = 85.5%. These describe different stages; do not swap denominators to make the result appear stronger. The unsold 38 units still need a documented disposition, date and quality assessment.

Assume six oven hours are available and each batch occupies 30 minutes: ten batches use five hours, leaving one hour. If each batch also requires 45 labor minutes, the work needs 7.5 labor hours. Seven available labor hours leave a 0.5-hour gap even though oven time fits. Cooling, packing, cleaning and simultaneous equipment limits can reduce practical output further.

The $4 price and $28 batch ingredient cost are fictional, not market averages. This deliberately charges ingredients for the whole production period, including unsold and rejected units. It is a bounded spending/contribution view rather than a formal inventory-valued income statement. Do not also deduct those same ingredients again when accounting for rejects.

The $375.40 remainder is before owner pay, tax, financing and omitted costs. An actual plan may need a different cost-of-sales and inventory treatment, equipment replacement and other expenses. Keep purchasing, consumption, production loss, sales and cash receipts as separate records. The market-stall plan adds an event selling-channel view; it is not a replacement for bakery production controls.

Test lower demand before raising production

If only 300 of the same 380 saleable units sell, revenue falls to $1,200 and sold-unit packaging to $90. Ingredients remain $280 because all ten batches were already made. With the same $300 labor, $60 utilities and $250 other costs, the remainder is $220. Unsold saleable units rise to 80. Lower sell-through does not automatically undo the earlier ingredient purchase or production work.

Suppose the original 342-unit scenario earns $1,368 but collects only $1,200 during the period, leaving $168 outstanding. With $2,000 opening operating cash and $992.60 of listed payments, closing cash is $2,207.40. Any unsold product is not cash in the bank; customer payment timing must also be checked.

Use a dated preorder and batch register to test the demand hypothesis, but do not assume orders are noncancelable or collected unless the evidence shows it. Compare actual sell-through by product and day. A smaller batch, different production day or reduced range may be a better next test than simply adding an oven.

Verify product requirements and preserve the evidence

The FDA food-business overview explains that requirements depend on the product and facility and involve relevant federal, state and local authorities. A private-residence registration exclusion is not blanket permission to sell any home-baked food. Confirm the actual operation's permissions, labeling and handling requirements with the responsible authorities; this worksheet does not inspect or certify them.

Create a plan with customer evidence, unit and batch definitions, production constraints, ingredient and packaging basis, loss records, costs, dated receipts/payments and a downside response. Keep sensitive customer and supplier records out of public notes. Use the business-plan guide to connect the sections and the editable template to record the narrative.

During a four-week permitted trial, retain the initial forecast and review actual batches, rejects, unsold units, labor hours and collections. Name an owner, review date and spending ceiling. Are all produced units sales? No. Does unused oven time prove spare production capacity? No; labor and other constraints may bind first. Is the remainder take-home profit? No; the cost boundary and omitted items remain explicit.
