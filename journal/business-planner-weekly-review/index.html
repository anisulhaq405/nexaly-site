Weekly Business Planner: Free Review Template and Example

By NexalyPlanner · Published September 15, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Back to the Nexaly Planner Journal

A business planner for a small business should turn records into weekly decisions. It does not need to copy every detail from bookkeeping, inventory and marketing tools. It needs a consistent view of what changed, what is due and what the owner will do next.

The framework below suits a solo owner or small team selling products or services. Adapt the fields to your operation and keep official accounting, tax and legal records in the systems required for those purposes.

Quick answer: review sales, expected cash, open orders, inventory or delivery capacity, marketing activity and three next actions. Use the same definitions and review date each week so comparisons remain meaningful.

For a business planning journal, add two short notes: “What changed, and what evidence explains it?” and “What decision will I check next week?” Keep observations separate from guesses. A quiet sales week does not prove a campaign failed if the order or attribution records are incomplete.

The U.S. Small Business Administration business guide separates planning, launching, managing and growing a business across distinct activities. A weekly planner is narrower: it keeps the operating plan alive between larger strategy or financial-planning exercises.

Bring monthly goals into the week without copying every record

Start the month with a few dated outcomes: a launch, a client delivery, an inventory order or a reporting deadline. At the weekly review, ask which milestone needs work now and reserve an actual block for it. The weekly digital planner example shows how to turn that decision into time blocks and handoffs. A planner can hold the decision and next action while the source details remain in your booking, inventory and accounting records.

For example, Maya runs a fictional tutoring business preparing a September study-skills workshop. Her monthly view holds the workshop date, registration-page deadline and room confirmation. This week she has five inquiry follow-ups, a form to test and a handout to review. She assigns a 45-minute form test on Tuesday, a room call on Wednesday and a one-hour review on Friday. She records inquiry status without counting an inquiry as a sale.

Set one weekly review time

Choose a time after the week’s main transactions are recorded but early enough to act on problems. Friday afternoon works for some businesses; Monday morning works for others. Use a stated cutoff such as “orders paid through Sunday 11:59 p.m. Eastern” so the numbers do not drift between reviews.

  1. Close or flag incomplete records.
  2. Compare the week with the prior week and the current target.
  3. Write the reason for any material change you can support.
  4. List blocked orders and near-term cash commitments.
  5. Assign three next actions with owners and due dates.

Worked example: one shop’s weekly review

Consider a fictional online shop. This week it recorded 42 paid orders and $3,780 in sales before refunds. Two refunds totaling $150 were processed. Thirty-six orders shipped, six remain open, and two of those are waiting for a low-stock item. The owner also knows that $1,400 of supplier and shipping payments are due within seven days.

Keep cash and profit separate

Sales do not equal cash, and cash does not equal profit. A card payment may settle later; inventory purchases may be paid before related products sell; tax collected may not belong to the business. Label every figure and period. Use qualified accounting advice for official statements and tax treatment.

For product businesses, connect the review to the sales and inventory tracking workflow. If stock timing is the issue, calculate a reorder point using demand, lead time and safety stock.

Common questions

Is a business planner the same as a business plan?

No. A business plan explains the model, market, strategy and financial expectations. A business planner supports recurring execution and review. The two can refer to each other.

Which numbers should a new business review weekly?

Start with a small set you can define and maintain: sales, available cash, upcoming payments, open commitments and the activity that produces demand. Add measures only when they change a decision.

Can I use a planner instead of bookkeeping software?

No. A planning view can summarize operating information, but it should not be treated as a replacement for complete accounting records or professional advice.

Example: a 20-minute weekly review for a solo business owner

Imagine a shop reports $3,000 in orders this week, but only $2,100 in payments actually received. It also has 12 open orders, three overdue supplier deliveries and one unexpected return. Those are different signals: orders are not necessarily cash, open orders are not fulfilled shipments and a returned item is not automatically sellable inventory.

Use the same five questions every Friday

  1. Cash: what was received, what was paid and which confirmed bills fall due before the next review?
  2. Orders: which customers are waiting, and who owns each next step?
  3. Stock: which item might run out before its next dependable delivery?
  4. Capacity: what is preventing the owner or team from completing promised work?
  5. Next week: what two or three outcomes matter more than a longer task list?

For example, if the business has $1,200 available cash and $950 in confirmed payments due next week, the simple pre-receipts cash buffer is $1,200 − $950 = $250. This is not a complete cash-flow forecast: expected receipts, operating costs and timing still need their own records. A good review ends with a named next action, owner and due date, such as “confirm supplier ETA by Monday noon.”

A 20-minute weekly owner review you can repeat

  1. Minutes 0–5: confirm the review date, available cash, bills due and the source of each number. Separate unpaid orders from received money.
  2. Minutes 5–10: scan overdue customer promises, stock exceptions and supplier delays. Choose issues that need a decision, not every low-priority notification.
  3. Minutes 10–15: identify the top one to three decisions for next week. Write the evidence still missing and avoid inventing a target with no basis.
  4. Minutes 15–20: assign each action a verb, owner and date. Schedule the next review and keep the record in your working system.

Weekly business planner versus a business plan template

A business plan documents who you serve, the offer and assumptions to test. A weekly business planner checks current sales, open work and next decisions against reality.
