Security Company Business Plan: Staffing, Coverage & Cash

By NexalyPlanner · Published November 6, 2026

Build a security company business plan around defined service scope, billable coverage, paid hours, relief staffing and payroll cash. Includes a fictional contract example.

Example basis: Businesses, amounts, fees and outcomes in the worked examples are fictional planning assumptions in USD, prepared November 6, 2026. They are not market averages, observed customer results or income promises.

Define a permitted service before pricing coverage

A security company business plan should define the actual service, jurisdiction, qualified staff, client responsibilities and operating boundary before discussing growth. This guide models a fictional, properly authorized unarmed site-coverage service. It provides business scheduling and cash examples, not operational security, use-of-force or weapons instructions.

Name the client type, coverage window, required reporting, escalation contact and acceptance evidence. A contract for a staffed post is different from mobile visits, investigations, alarm services or personal protection. Do not reuse one hourly margin across services with different qualifications, travel, equipment and supervision requirements. Verify what the company and each worker may lawfully provide.

Billing 320 hours does not mean paying only 320 hours. Document the actual handoff, travel, training, supervision, absence and relief arrangements. Two people each available for 40 hours provide exactly 80 hours before any absence; there is no spare coverage. A roster must satisfy the actual location, worker qualifications, timing and applicable employment rules, not just a monthly total.

Allocate named, appropriately qualified coverage to each shift and identify the substitute before promising the contract. The model does not determine lawful shift lengths, breaks, overtime, employment status or minimum pay. Use the actual requirements and qualified professional guidance where needed.

The $32 fee and $22 staffing cost are hypothetical, not market rates or wage advice. Fully loaded means that the example input is intended to include the staffing cost components you identify; it is not proof that every required component has been included. Add actual payroll burdens, insurance, travel and administration to the relevant line without counting them twice.

If an absence requires eight incremental paid relief hours at the same $22 cost, the extra $176 reduces the modeled remainder to $748. This scenario explicitly assumes an additional cost; an actual substitution may have a different payment effect. Keep that difference visible rather than treating relief capacity as free.

Fund payroll timing before accepting the work

The four-week staffing cost is $7,216, or $1,804 each modeled week. If all $9,316 of listed costs are paid before the first client receipt and opening cash is $9,000, the projected trough is negative $316. That is an unfunded gap, not permission to run a negative bank balance. Establish actual funding, change the commitment or revise agreed payment timing before starting.

Receiving the full $10,240 later would produce a mathematical end balance of $9,924 from the original $9,000, but that later balance does not solve the earlier inability to make payments. A monthly profit calculation can conceal a weekly payroll problem. Build a dated forecast and allow a downside where the receipt arrives later than expected.

Reconcile scheduled hours, confirmed coverage, approved timesheets, billed hours and collected invoices separately. A filled roster is not proof that work occurred, and a client invoice is not a cash receipt. Keep sensitive staff, incident and client information in appropriately controlled systems; the public plan needs only aggregated assumptions.

Use licensing and contract evidence as release gates

The Texas Department of Public Safety regulates private security in Texas, including armed and unarmed roles. Use its official guidance for a Texas operation and the responsible regulator elsewhere. This plan neither grants a license nor establishes that a worker or company is eligible for a particular assignment.

Before a contract starts, verify the permitted service, company and individual credentials, required training, insurance, written scope, staffing availability and payment terms. Assign a review owner and record expiry dates where relevant. Keep an honest stop condition: unresolved permissions, uncovered shifts or unfunded payroll must not be marked ready merely because the forecast shows revenue.

Use the startup checklist for evidence and owners, and the business-plan template for the narrative. The business-plan guide links the wider planning process. Is the hourly spread profit? No. Can monthly availability prove every shift is covered? No; validate the dated roster and substitutes.
