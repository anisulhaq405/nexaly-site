Construction Company Business Plan: Milestone Cash Example

By NexalyPlanner · Published October 13, 2026

Fictional worked examples

A construction company business plan should show when project money arrives relative to materials and labor payments. A profitable quote can still require cash before the next milestone is collected. This guide focuses on the operating example named in the title; use it as one supporting part of your wider business plan.

Define the project and the collection assumptions

Choose one project, its expected start/end and the cash available for that project. Identify the scope, customer, supplier dates and delivery dependencies in the wider plan. Enter receipts on their expected collection dates, not invoice issue dates. Record a dependency such as customer approval, inspection or invoice acceptance in the action note. The schedule tracks an assumption; it does not establish a contractual right to a deposit or milestone payment.

Build the fictional four-movement schedule

Opening project cash is $1,000. A confirmed $2,000 deposit on October 13 raises day-end cash to $3,000. A $2,500 materials payment on October 14 leaves $500.

An expected $1,200 labor payment on October 16 takes cash to negative $700. An expected $3,000 milestone collection on October 23 restores it to $2,300. The project closes positive in this example but has a visible earlier gap. A closing total alone would miss it.

Compare confirmed receipts with all commitments

The base schedule includes every entered receipt and payment. The downside removes receipts marked Expected while retaining all payments, including expected payments. In the example, the October 23 collection drops out and downside closing remains negative $700. Do not remove an uncertain payment just to make the balance positive; you need to understand when it can genuinely change. A Confirmed label should reflect current evidence, and the owner should revisit it if a customer or dependency changes.

Make a decision before the lowest cash date

Identify which commitment creates the gap and what evidence can change the timing. An owner can verify the collection date, discuss feasible sequencing with the relevant parties or revise the work scope in the appropriate contract process. The worksheet does not choose financing or give legal terms. Record a specific next action and preserve the original schedule. Recalculate only after changing an actual input, so the reason for an improved scenario remains visible.

Use the project schedule beside the company cash plan

One project can look funded while several concurrent projects compete for the same bank balance. Do not allocate the same opening cash independently to every project and then add their results as company cash. Consolidate receipts and payments in the existing cash forecast. Same-day movements are grouped in this worksheet, so an outgoing morning payment before an afternoon receipt can create an intraday shortage the day-end balance will not reveal.

Questions about milestone cash planning

Does Expected mean included? Yes in the base schedule; the confirmed-receipts downside excludes only expected receipts. Can I track several projects?

Keep separate project reports and use a consolidated company forecast to avoid double-counting cash. Is the final cash balance project profit? No; opening funds, deposits and payment timing make cash and profit different measures.

What changes if the milestone receipt is late?

Keep the fictional deposit and payments unchanged but move the $3,000 collection from October 23 to October 30. With no other movements, cash stays at negative $700 from October 16 through October 29, then rises to $2,300 on October 30. The lowest balance is unchanged, but the funding gap lasts seven additional days. Include October 30 in the planning window to see the receipt; do not treat the later positive balance as proof that earlier commitments are funded.
