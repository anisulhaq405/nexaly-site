A Black Friday marketing plan for a small business should connect the offer, available stock, content, checkout and fulfillment. More posts will not solve a campaign in which customers cannot identify the eligible items or complete an order.

For the 2026 US campaign, use Friday, November 27 as Black Friday and Monday, November 30 as Cyber Monday. The dates follow Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday in November, as identified in the US Office of Personnel Management holiday guidance. The workback dates below are an original planning example, not an official retail schedule.

Decide what the campaign is supposed to accomplish

Choose a concrete commercial objective you can evaluate: sell a selected seasonal collection, introduce a new customer to a core product, or promote a clearly defined bundle. Record the offer's scope before writing captions.

For a clothing boutique, scope includes the eligible SKUs and variants. A product family can look well stocked while a commonly requested size has only one unit available. Review variant-level availability before promising broad selection.

Select a simple offer your team can explain consistently. If a discount applies only to selected items, make that limitation visible wherever the offer is described. Work out the actual cost and margin implications using your own records before approving the promotion.

Write an offer brief before writing the content

Your internal brief should state what is included, the price or discount method, any exclusions, the start and end times, and the timezone. Add the destination URL and the person who approves changes.

For a nationwide US audience, write a specific timezone when a deadline matters. A phrase such as “ends at midnight” leaves customers in different timezones uncertain.

Avoid showing a discount in one place while leaving a different price or condition in checkout. Recheck every customer-facing location after an offer changes. Keep an earlier version of the brief so the team can resolve questions about what was advertised.

Give each campaign phase a different content job

Before launch, explain the featured products and answer practical questions. Show measurements, available colors, actual item details and where to find delivery information. This work can remain useful after the promotion ends.

At launch, make the offer easy to understand. Lead customers to the eligible collection or product page, not a destination that forces them to search again. Identify the item and variant clearly in the creative.

During the campaign, answer the questions coming from real shoppers. Publish accurate availability updates and explain any changed delivery information. Use deadline reminders only when they correspond to a genuine end time.

After the campaign, close or update expired promotional messages and keep helping customers with their orders. A sold-out landing page can offer accurate next steps, such as currently available alternatives, without implying the original item remains available.

Test the customer path on a phone

Open the offer as a customer would. Check that the product page loads, variants can be selected, pricing is clear, and the checkout button goes to the correct destination. Test the relevant delivery and payment options through your store's supported testing process.

Confirm what happens after a successful purchase: the order confirmation, internal order record and fulfillment handoff. For a digital product, include the download-delivery step in that test.

Also inspect an unavailable variant and an invalid discount code. Error messages should help a shopper recover. A campaign checklist that contains only successful paths misses problems that actual customers can encounter.

Prepare a small exception queue for launch day

Track paid orders that are not moving, partially fulfilled orders, stock discrepancies and questions about the offer. Give each exception an owner and next action.

For a boutique, a manual order log can help if information arrives through more than one channel. The small-business order tracker explains how to separate payment and fulfillment. Keep one authoritative order record so two people do not send conflicting updates.

Review results using the same definitions

Compare product visits, completed orders, collected payments, refunds and relevant campaign costs over a stated period. Distinguish an order placed from an order paid or fulfilled. Record how you attributed a sale to the campaign and where attribution is uncertain.

Keep a note of practical findings: a repeated sizing question, a variant that sold out, a confusing offer term or a packing delay. Those details can improve the next campaign even when the headline sales figure looks good.

Frequently asked questions

When should a small business start planning Black Friday?

Start early enough to approve the offer, create accurate content and test the customer path. The six-week workback here is an example that can be adapted to your inventory and production capacity.

Does every small business need a large discount?

No. The offer needs to make commercial sense and be explained accurately. Evaluate selected products, genuine bundles or another clearly defined proposition using your actual costs and customer needs.

Should the promotion continue through Cyber Monday?

Only if that continuation is part of an approved plan. Keep the advertised end time consistent with what customers can actually buy.

Can I use this plan next year?

Yes, after updating the real campaign dates, products, terms and lessons from the previous run. Review the substance of the plan rather than changing only the year in the title.
