Test lot history and offline handoffs when your work requires them

A maker may need more than SKU counts. Build a non-sensitive test with one source lot used in two production batches and one batch containing two source lots. Ship some finished units, hold others and record a return. Ask the tool to trace both directions and explain the quantity still on site. The batch and lot log worked example gives a small, reconcilable dataset. A total count that looks correct does not prove source and destination links survive.

A rental owner has a different test: block one unit for maintenance, then attempt to plan a booking inside the hold. Review whether the conflict remains visible and what staff must do to resolve it. Use the rental maintenance and booking example to distinguish service history from customer availability. Neither lot genealogy nor rental scheduling should be assumed from the word “inventory.”

Assign ownership of a browser-local working copy

Suppose two staff members open separate local files on separate devices. If those copies do not synchronize, a receipt entered on one device will not automatically appear on the other. Write down which record is authoritative, who makes changes and how others receive an approved current view. A license allowing business use does not by itself create multiuser collaboration.

For a recovery test, retain the current backup, restore a sample into an isolated workspace, and check a known item, its movements and linked records. Opening a CSV proves that text can be read; it may not reconstruct lots, orders or settings. Record the version and export format used. If simultaneous editing, remote approvals or automatic storefront imports are essential, verify those capabilities explicitly before choosing an offline workflow. A one-time price should not hide a mismatch with the way your team works.
