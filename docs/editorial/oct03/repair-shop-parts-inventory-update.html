Close a reservation without deducting stock twice

Use a separate sample to test the boundary between a reservation and a physical issue. The shop begins with 10 air filters, reserves three for WO-310 and two for WO-311, then receives four more. Physical stock is 14, open reservations are five, and available stock is nine. WO-310 then consumes its three filters. Physical stock falls to 11 and its three-unit reservation closes; the remaining two-unit reservation means available stock is still nine. Subtracting the consumed quantity from stock while retaining all five reservations would incorrectly show only six available.

A cancellation releases a hold; it does not create a receipt. If one unused filter physically returns from WO-310 and passes the shop's condition review, record a one-unit return against that work order. Physical and available stock become 12. If the filter is damaged, retain it in the appropriate non-sellable record instead. Record each transition and its reference once.

Match the parts issue to the final job

At an illustrative $8 unit cost, the original three-filter issue totals $24. A valid one-filter unused return reduces net consumption to two filters, or $16 under this simple unchanged-cost assumption. Check the final work-order cost and customer invoice against actual usage. Inventory, billing and formal accounting can have different rules; a corrected stock count is not permission to change an invoice silently.

Make these events part of a weekly exception review: issued parts with no job ID, canceled jobs with active reservations, unexplained positive adjustments and returned parts awaiting inspection. If workshop instruments also affect service decisions, keep their evidence in a separate equipment calibration log; parts stock does not prove a measuring instrument is suitable for use.
