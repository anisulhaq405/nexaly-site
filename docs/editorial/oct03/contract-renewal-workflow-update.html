Keep amendments and owner handoffs visible

Extend the fictional SUP-017 example with a documented amendment: the original register says 60 calendar days before December 31, 2026, but an authorized reviewer finds a later signed amendment using 90 calendar days. For this example's stated arithmetic, December 31 minus 90 days is October 2, 2026, rather than November 1. Record the old value, amended rule, source section, reviewer and verification date. Do not just replace “60” with “90” and remove the reason for the change.

If the discrepancy is discovered after October 2, label the issue for urgent qualified review. Changing the internal decision date cannot establish that a contractual deadline was met. The controlling text, delivery rule and relevant advice determine the response. These dates are a hypothetical calculation, not an interpretation of anyone's actual agreement.

For an owner handoff, name the next action and its due date: “review the renewal quotation by October 8” is more useful than “new owner informed.” Keep an internal review task separate from notice delivery and supplier acknowledgment. A sent-message timestamp may not establish receipt; retain the evidence your agreement requires.

If a renewed supplier term changes payment dates, carry the agreed amounts and bank timing into the 13-week cash forecast. Renewal status and cash availability answer different questions.
