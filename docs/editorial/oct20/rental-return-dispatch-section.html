Recheck the next dispatch after a late return

A planned return time is not an actual release time. In this separate fictional example, LIGHT-07 is due back at 09:00 UTC but arrives at 10:15. The inspection and preparation are completed at 11:00, with no unresolved findings in this example. Loading takes another 30 minutes. The earliest planned dispatch is therefore 11:30 UTC. A customer expecting dispatch at 11:15 faces a 15-minute conflict, even though both bookings appear on different lines in the calendar.

One asset, one date; all example times are UTC
MilestoneRecorded timeBooking decision
Expected return09:00A planning assumption until the asset arrives
Actual return10:15Record receipt; keep the unit unavailable pending checks
Inspection and preparation complete11:00Record the result and responsible person's release decision
Loading complete / earliest dispatch11:30Reconcile this with the promised 11:15 dispatch

The booking owner can review a suitable available replacement, agree a revised dispatch with the customer, or record that the original commitment cannot be met. Keep the customer's agreed outcome with the booking. Do not change the original promised time in the record without retaining the change and its reason.

If inspection instead finds an unresolved problem, 11:30 is no longer an available slot. Keep release pending and assign the next review. An estimated service finish is useful for planning, but it does not establish that the asset passed its required checks.

Keep a short handoff record

  • Asset ID and outgoing booking reference.
  • Expected return, actual return and the timezone used.
  • Inspection result, release evidence and responsible person.
  • Remaining preparation/loading time and proposed dispatch.
  • Affected customer promise, agreed resolution and next owner.
