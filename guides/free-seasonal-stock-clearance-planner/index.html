Seasonal Stock Clearance Planner: inputs, example and review steps

Updated November 14, 2026. Open the free worksheet.

How to plan a seasonal stock clearance

Use this free clearance calculator to compare the cash and contribution consequences of a proposed markdown. It suits seasonal clothing, accessories and other nonperishable retail stock. It does not choose the best discount, predict customer demand or change prices in your store.

  1. Count only sellable, unreserved units for each SKU, size and color. Use the size and color stock matrix before planning a broken size run.
  2. Choose a selling window and enter a realistic number of expected sold units. Start with your own recent sales, promotion plans and remaining season; do not assume a discount guarantees a sellout.
  3. Enter the current price, proposed markdown and landed inventory cost per unit. The shipment cost allocator can help distribute shared freight before this review.
  4. Add percentage selling fees, extra selling cost per sold unit and fixed costs for this SKU scenario. Allocate shared campaign costs once, rather than repeating the full campaign amount on every row.
  5. Calculate, compare discounts, record a review date, then save or export the scenario before testing another assumption.

Worked example: 20 totes available, 10 expected sales

All inputs here are fictional. Current price is $30, markdown is 20%, inventory cost is $12, selling fee is 5%, extra selling cost is $1 per sold unit, and fixed scenario costs are $15. The window is four weeks.

  • Clearance unit price: $30 × (1 − 20%) = $24.
  • Sales revenue: 10 × $24 = $240.
  • Percentage fees: $240 × 5% = $12; extra selling costs: 10 × $1 = $10.
  • Net sale proceeds before inventory cost: $240 − $12 − $10 − $15 = $203.
  • Contribution after sold inventory cost: $203 − (10 × $12) = $83. This is not net profit.
  • Remaining stock: 20 − 10 = 10 units, at entered cost of $120. Clearing those in four weeks would require at least 3 units per week, rounded up, in addition to the modeled sales.

At the same 10 sold units, a 40% markdown gives an $18 unit price, $146 net sale proceeds and $26 contribution. It leaves the same 10 units unsold because sales volume has not changed. Test a different expected quantity separately to examine a different demand assumption.

Cash recovery, gross margin and contribution mean different things

Net sale proceeds deduct entered selling costs but not inventory cost: it represents proceeds from this sale scenario, before settlement timing or unpaid suppliers. Contribution also deducts the entered cost of sold units. Gross margin is (revenue − sold inventory cost) ÷ revenue, before selling costs. None of these establishes business net profit. The unsold stock balance remains separate; this worksheet does not expense all available inventory as though it sold.

Zero-contribution price and rounding

The floor is the lowest whole-cent price that covers sold inventory cost, percentage fees, extra unit costs and allocated fixed costs at the same entered sales volume. The example floor is $15.27. At $15.26, contribution is −$0.03; at $15.27 it is $0.07 after row-level fee rounding. A floor cannot be established when expected sales are zero, or when selling fees are 100% and entered costs are positive. When all entered costs are zero, the floor is zero. A floor is a planning reference, not a recommended selling price.

The tool rounds unit price to cents before multiplying by sold units. It rounds percentage fees once per SKU row. Real platforms may round fees per transaction, apply tax differently or charge additional fees, so reconcile against your actual terms.

Choose the next action by SKU

Before cutting every price, review weak product presentation, missing sizes, seasonal timing and fulfillment cost. Consider a limited markdown test, a clearly priced bundle, a supplier return where allowed, a wholesale lot, or carrying suitable evergreen stock forward. Record any storage, handling or campaign cost needed for a fair comparison. Donation, disposal and tax outcomes need separate records and are not calculated here.

Shopify’s retail markdown guide explains markdowns and inventory carrying costs. The calculations on this page are our transparent hypothetical examples, not industry discount benchmarks. No universal clearance percentage applies to every boutique.

Frequently asked questions

What is a seasonal stock clearance planner?

It is a manual worksheet for comparing a proposed markdown with expected sales, selling costs, inventory cost and remaining stock before you approve a clearance action.

Does a 30% discount guarantee more sales?

No. Discount comparisons hold expected sold units constant. Enter a different expected quantity to test another assumption; this is not a price elasticity or demand forecasting model.

Can I model only part of my stock selling?

Yes. Enter available units and expected sold units separately. Expected sales cannot exceed available units. Blank expected sales preserves the original assumption that all entered units sell; enter 0 explicitly to model no sales.

Is contribution the same as net profit?

No. Contribution deducts entered sold inventory cost and selling costs. Unentered overhead, tax, refunds and other business costs are excluded.

What happens to stock that does not sell?

The report shows remaining units and their entered inventory cost separately. It does not assume disposal, a future sale, an accounting write-down or automatic replenishment.

Can I restore an older backup?

Yes. Version 1 backups from this same tool retain their original all-units-sell assumption, zero extra unit selling costs and a four-week default window. Review the new fields before using the results.

Input rules and recovery

Each worksheet supports up to 100 unique references. Required numbers are nonnegative and money and percentages accept up to two decimals. Available and sold quantities are whole units, up to 1,000,000; expected sold cannot exceed available. The optional selling window accepts 1–52 whole weeks, defaulting to four when blank. The date is a review label, not an automated deadline.

Use one currency consistently; no conversion is performed. Blank expected sold units means all available units sell. Enter 0 explicitly for no sales. Blank extra cost per sold unit means zero. Other scenario costs apply once per row. Sales assumptions are not validated against your real inventory or customers.

Save on this device stores editable data in this browser. JSON backup supports editable recovery; CSV and PDF are reports. Imports show a preview before replacing the current worksheet and do not overwrite the device save until you choose Save. Older version 1 backups from this tool remain supported with the legacy defaults described above. Browser-data clearing can remove device saves; keep JSON backups separately.
