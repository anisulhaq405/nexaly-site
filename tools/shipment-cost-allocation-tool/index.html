How to allocate shipment costs across SKUs

A shipment cost allocation spreads a shared invoice across the products that caused it. This calculator separates merchandise cost, shared charges and direct line costs, so you can review the cost per received unit before updating your own inventory or pricing records.

  1. Identify the shipment. Add a label, review date and currency. Convert invoices into that currency before entering them; changing the currency label does not convert amounts.
  2. Separate cost pools. Enter freight, insurance, handling and other eligible shared amounts once. If your old worksheet stored every charge in “Shared shipment charges,” that amount remains in the freight/shared field: separate it before also filling the new cost pools.
  3. Enter received product lines. Give each line a unique reference, SKU, positive whole-unit quantity and net merchandise unit cost. Use a separate reference when the same SKU has multiple receipt lines.
  4. Choose each charge’s driver. Freight may follow packed weight or volume; insurance may follow value. Enter kg and CBM per received unit. The calculator multiplies each by quantity. Use the same units throughout.
  5. Add actual direct costs. Put a known charge attributable to one SKU, such as its separately established duty amount, in that line’s direct cost. Do not also include it in a shared pool.
  6. Calculate and reconcile. Review the SKU report, charge-pool reconciliation and alternative-method table. Record the invoice and reason for your policy, then save or export.

Shipment allocation formula and cent rounding

Line share = line basis ÷ total basis.
Unrounded allocation = cost pool × line share.
Landed line total = merchandise line cost + allocated shared charges + direct line costs.
Estimated unit cost = landed line total ÷ received units.
Cost uplift = (allocated shared charges + direct cost) ÷ merchandise line cost × 100%.

Each cost pool is allocated independently in integer cents. The calculator floors each line’s exact share, then distributes the remaining cents to the largest fractional remainders. Ties follow input row order. Allocating $0.01 equally to three lines gives $0.01, $0.00 and $0.00; the shared invoice still reconciles exactly. All supported currencies use two decimal places here.

Keep the exact landed line total as your control: a rounded unit display may not multiply back to it. For example, $10.00 divided by three units displays $3.33, whose multiplication yields $9.99. This is display rounding, not an unallocated freight cent. Weight and volume inputs allow up to six decimal places; money inputs allow two.

What belongs in landed cost?

Include only costs relevant to your chosen internal cost scope. IAS 2 describes inventory cost in terms of purchase, conversion and costs of bringing inventory to its present location and condition. Its purchase-cost guidance distinguishes recoverable taxes and supplier discounts. Use your applicable accounting policy to decide which entered costs belong in inventory; this worksheet does not make that recognition decision.

Customs value is a separate calculation. For example, US CBP explains that a CIF price including freight and insurance is not itself the value to declare for CBP purposes. Do not submit this calculator’s landed total as a universal customs value. There is no HS classification, duty-rate lookup, tax calculation, live freight quote or exchange-rate feed.

Prevent the most common allocation errors

  • Double-counted delivery: if the supplier price already includes a separately entered charge, separate the invoice consistently first.
  • Wrong measurement scale: do not enter a whole-line kg or CBM total into the per-unit field.
  • Mixed receipts: allocate costs over the actual receipt scope. A partial receipt and a full purchase order are different denominators.
  • Inapplicable duties: record SKU-specific duties as direct actual amounts rather than spreading one rate across unrelated products.
  • Recoverable taxes or selling costs: review the policy before including them in inventory cost. Do not assume every cash payment is eligible.
  • Silent free shares: for a nonzero pool, every line must have positive data for its selected basis. A missing value never silently becomes an equal split.

Shipment cost allocation FAQs

How do I divide shipping cost between products?

Choose the relevant driver, calculate each line’s share of the total driver, and multiply the freight bill by that share. This tool supports value, units, packed weight, volume and equal-per-line allocation, with cent reconciliation.

Can I use different methods for freight and insurance?

Yes. Choose a separate basis for freight, insurance, handling and other shared charges. The pool breakdown shows both the selected methods and the reconciled allocations.

Is weight or value allocation more accurate?

The appropriate method follows the cost driver and your policy. Weight may explain a weight-priced freight invoice, while value may explain insurance. The method comparison exposes the effect on each SKU; it does not certify a method.

Can I enter customs duty by SKU?

Yes, as a known actual amount in that line’s direct-cost field. It is a whole-line charge, not a unit cost or percentage. Obtain the amount separately and do not duplicate it in a shared pool.

Can I import Excel and save a PDF?

Export product rows as CSV from your spreadsheet, then paste the CSV text. Direct XLSX upload is not supported. Use Print / Save PDF for a report and JSON backup for editable recovery.

Why is a weight or volume comparison unavailable?

Every product line needs a positive per-unit measurement for that method. Enter the missing data to compare it. A missing comparison does not block a different, fully specified chosen method.

Is this a full landed cost or customs calculator?

It calculates landed line estimates from the goods, shared charges and direct costs you enter. It does not discover missing costs, classify goods, calculate tariffs, determine customs value or decide accounting treatment.
