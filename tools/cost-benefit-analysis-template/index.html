Original fictional example

Over 12 months, A costs $1,200 +12 × $100 = $2,400 and has $3,000 entered benefits, leaving $600 net benefit. B costs $600 +12 × $150 = $2,400 and has $2,880 benefits, leaving $480. A exceeds B by $120 under these assumptions. With benefits at $150 and $140 monthly instead, net benefits become negative $600 and negative $720. Neither is automatically a good purchase.

Set a fair comparison boundary

Name the decision and fix the same 1–120 whole-month horizon for both options. The start, end and review dates label your decision-review record; the separate month count controls the calculation horizon and is not inferred from those dates. Include implementation and ongoing costs that change between them. Each money field accepts 0–1,000,000 USD with at most two decimals. Enter zero deliberately when a category is absent. Costs are nonnegative; the net result can be negative.

Both options need a credible baseline. If doing nothing costs money, document it as one option rather than assuming zero. Keep each estimate's source and date in the required assumptions note. The worksheet uses flat monthly amounts across the entire horizon; it does not model phased adoption or annual-only bills.

Understand the formulas

Total cost = one-time cost + months × monthly cost. Total entered benefit = months × monthly benefit. Net benefit = total benefit minus total cost. A-minus-B compares net benefits; it does not rank quality, risk or feasibility. No discount rate, inflation, tax, financing or terminal value is included. A benefit-cost ratio and payback date are not calculated.

If you value saved time, say how hours and the assumed hourly value were chosen. Released capacity is not automatically a cash saving: payroll or other spending must actually change before describing it that way. Do not count both higher sales and the entire same sales margin as separate benefits. Do not add an avoided cost as a benefit while also subtracting it from costs without explaining the baseline.

Use the result as a review prompt

Run a lower-benefit scenario and preserve both versions. Record nonmonetary quality, accessibility, migration effort and dependency risks alongside the amounts. An option with a higher entered net benefit can still be infeasible. The business-plan software comparison includes a practical acceptance exercise; the strategic-plan worksheet turns a reviewed choice into owned actions.

Questions before deciding

Are benefits cash receipts? Only if the entered assumption actually represents cash; label imputed value separately. Does a positive net benefit justify buying? It only describes these entries and exclusions. Can I compare uneven years or discounted cash flows? No; use an appropriately reviewed model for those cases.
