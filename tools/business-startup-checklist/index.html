Fields and limits

Enter a project name (up to 100 characters) and a fixed review date. Add 1–30 actions, each with a task (200 characters), owner (80), optional real due date, importance, status and evidence/reason (500). Marking Done requires evidence text; Not applicable requires a reason. The worksheet does not inspect or authenticate the evidence.

How the report is calculated

Applicable required actions exclude rows you mark Not applicable. Required done divided by applicable required gives the displayed percentage. Open required actions are applicable required rows not marked Done. Overdue means an open required row has a due date before the fixed review date; a task due on that date is not counted overdue. If no applicable required actions exist, no completion percentage is shown and readiness is not established.

Business startup checklist PDF: what to do before launch

Download the free three-page business startup checklist PDF for a practical action list, a blank owner-and-evidence worksheet and a filled fictional example. It uses US Letter pages and requires no signup. Print the blank sheet for handwriting, or use the browser checklist above to record up to 30 actions and export a reviewed report.

The PDF is static, not fillable. A checkbox records your decision; it does not verify that a requirement is satisfied. The browser version requires an owner and evidence before an action can be marked Done.

Work through these six preparation areas

  1. Customer and offer: describe the buyer, problem, price and service or product scope. Record the evidence that supports the demand assumption.
  2. Business model and cash: separate one-time startup costs, recurring costs, owner time and dated receipts. Decide how the business funds a slower first month.
  3. Requirements to verify: identify the structure, name, location, registration, permits, tax, insurance and other questions that apply to your actual activity. Record the responsible official source and next action; requirements differ by place and business.
  4. Delivery: confirm supplier terms, stock or working capacity, quality checks, fulfillment and the handling of cancellations or returns.
  5. Records and payments: decide who maintains records, reconciles payments, controls access and restores a backup.
  6. Launch review: assign unresolved actions, run a small delivery test and decide which blockers must be resolved before accepting a real commitment.

Turn a vague task into a checkable action

“Sort out cash” is hard to review. A clearer fictional action is “record the first month’s supplier payments and expected receipt dates,” with an owner, due date and a dated forecast reference. Use the cash forecast example for payment timing and the business-plan template for the wider narrative. Keep confidential documents outside public notes; a short reference is usually enough here.

Completion percentage is not launch approval

In the six-action sample, five actions are applicable and required, two are Done, and three remain open: 2 ÷ 5 = 40%. One optional action is Not applicable. If you mark a third required action Done with evidence, progress becomes 60%, but a blocked requirement may still prevent the next commitment. Optional work cannot compensate for an unresolved required action.

Choose PDF, browser checklist or business plan

  • PDF: a paper reference and handwritten action log. It does not calculate completion or save typed changes.
  • Browser checklist: assign owners, review dates, statuses and evidence; keep editable work through a same-tool JSON backup.
  • Business plan: explain the customer, offer, operating model and financial assumptions. It is a different document from a task checklist.

For US launch topics, start with the SBA launch guide and check the relevant responsible authority. This general planning worksheet does not determine legal, tax or licensing obligations.
