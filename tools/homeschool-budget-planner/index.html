Plan a homeschool budget without counting shared costs twice

Choose a project label, fixed review date, period length from 1 to 12 months and a USD funding budget. Add 1–50 items across curriculum, materials, activities, technology and other costs. Use anonymous learner labels or a shared household group. Enter a shared subscription once; do not repeat its full price for every child unless you really buy separate subscriptions.

What each row means

Quantity is the number of units per occurrence, from 1 to 100. Unit cost is the cost of one unit. Once means one occurrence in the whole period; monthly multiplies by your selected number of months. Paid to date is the actual total paid for that row so far, not a unit amount. Money entries accept nonnegative USD amounts up to $1,000,000 with at most two decimal places. This short worksheet does not convert currencies or track payment due dates.

Formulas and the budget boundary

Planned row cost = quantity × unit cost × occurrences. Occurrences are 1 for once, or the selected months for monthly. Total planned cost adds the rows. Planned headroom = funding budget minus planned cost. Budget remaining after payments = funding budget minus recorded payments. Planned cost not yet spent = planned minus paid; a negative value means payments exceed that plan. Actual payments may cover only part of the period, so this difference is not final savings. An entered funding budget is not a verified receipt and these numbers are not a bank balance.

Original fictional example

For three months and a $900 budget: two $120 curriculum packs cost $240 once; two $15 materials units per month cost $90; a shared $30 monthly activity costs $90; one shared $20 monthly subscription costs $60. Total planned cost is $480 and planned headroom $420. Payments of $240, $30, $30 and $20 total $320. Budget remaining after payments is $580; planned cost not yet spent is $160. These are invented entries, not a typical family budget or a recommendation to buy products.

Review before purchasing

Record what is included, renewals, delivery costs and the reason a shared item is needed. Update estimates from actual information and retain the original version. A negative planned headroom is a decision to revise the plan or confirm additional resources, not permission to spend. No grants, tax treatment, curriculum quality or homeschool compliance are evaluated by this tool.

Read the homeschool budget report before committing money

Use a fixed period that matches the costs you are planning. A three-month plan is not a full-year estimate: once-only purchases stay once-only, while monthly costs change when you edit the period. Confirm delivery, renewals and any costs left outside the worksheet before approving a purchase.

Shared cost or individual cost?

Suppose two learners share one $20 monthly subscription for three months. Record one subscription × $20 × three months = $60. Two genuinely separate subscriptions would cost $120. The learner label explains who uses the item; it does not automatically split a row or create another charge. Keep the provider's actual license terms beside your plan.

Reconcile the example after one more payment

The fictional plan totals $480 against a $900 funding budget, with $320 paid. If you pay another $50 toward already planned materials, recorded payments become $370. Budget remaining after payments becomes $530 and planned cost not yet spent becomes $110. Planned headroom stays $420 because the plan itself has not changed. Updating paid-to-date does not create a new planned purchase.

If the $50 is a new item rather than payment for an existing row, add the item and its cost first. Otherwise the report understates the plan. Neither an underspend nor a larger budget is evidence of educational progress.

Questions about the free worksheet

Is this a monthly or annual homeschool budget?

You choose a period of 1–12 months. Monthly rows repeat for that period; once-only rows occur once. Label the period clearly so the report is not mistaken for a calendar-year total.

Does paid-to-date change the planned cost?

No. Payments and the cost plan are separate inputs. Revise quantities or prices when the plan changes, and revise paid-to-date when a payment is made.

Can I use the worksheet for several children?

Yes. Use anonymous learner labels and enter shared household costs once. The worksheet does not automatically divide shared costs among learners.

Does the funding budget mean money is in my account?

No. It is an entered planning limit, not a verified receipt or bank balance. Check available funds and payment due dates separately.
